## This is just a brief introduction to exr and .hdr files and how they are different and also when to use what?

Both .hdr and .exr files are not real world files, just files that carry lighting information about a given scene. The differences are as follows:


SO the first thing to know about .hdr and .exr is that both of them store IMAGES - yes that can be a bit surprising but that is the hard truth of life - the most complicated things sometimes are too simple.

But the difference between a normal image and a HDRI or .exr images is that normal images have integer pixel values (0-255) for each channel and hence the effect of a pixel being brighter than another pixel is lost, For instance the sun is 255 in value, and some other portion - maybe the sky is also 255 - and we lose a sense of how bright the sun actually is.

Instead .hdr and .exr images store floating point values that represent brightness or intensity via floating point numbers without any ceiling. These values essentially represent how much light arrived at the camera from that point in the image or the direction and because there is no ceiling the values here are real relative intensity values so the ratio between the pixels are preserved.

Another good thing to know here is the way in which these .hdri and .exr images are generated - they are basically 3d worlds captured as a 360 degrees and then flattened out as an image. Hence there is a nice term associated with them - equirectangular which basically means that every pixel is mapped to a specific location in the world but regardless of what point in space each pixel links too, the distance between each pixel is still the same. And because of that, when the pole positions are located on the flattened image - they look distorted. Another thing to keep in mind is that this mapping is angles to pixels and hence in the .hdri image, every pixel is essentially mapped to a direction and it's value does not just represent intensity values, but also what direction is the light coming from?

Also, when rednering the scene in 3D - the renderer will treat every pixel as a light source at infinity, and in trying to shade each pixels, the "cycles" query what is the amount of light being recieved from all the directione before deciding the final intensity.

Most of the properties are shared between the .hdr and the .exr files - the only difference is how the information is packed into the two files. 

The .hdr files are a measurements at a single point, so it can't really deterministically calculate the effect of all the light sources on a given point. 

And the light sources are treated as being inifinitely far away so that the influence is always from a certain direction and never from a point.

The issues that we encountered while using .hdr with existing worlds and scenes:

- The structure of the given world or environment won't match with the given .hdri backdrop and the actual environment are not along the same camera heights and angles.
- Scale is another issue.
- For issues related to lighting, direction of the sun versus direction of the shadow.
- Double lighting - certainly not the case atleast in the case of blender pipeline.
- Strength and color of the HDRI expose.
- .hdr (Radiance RGBE)
