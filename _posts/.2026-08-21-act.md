Behaviou cloning and imitation learning - A trained professional / human performs a particular task n number of times recording both the actions and the outcomes -> Which are then used by the policy to learn to do the task.

Typical issues that exist with BC and Imitation Learning - Compounding errors that aggregate over multiple timesteps. This particularly happens here because the idea is simple - behavioural cloning happens on data collected through experts - i.e. an operator was in control throughout the entire process. Now when it's the robot or the agent, it is a possibility that the robot ends up making a small mistake and then ends up in a state that was never present in the training data - and eventually fails to recover. So a promising approach in this direction is the DAgger pathway - which simply does this:

- Trains a policy on a collected dataset.
- Lets the trained policy control the system based on whatever it has learnt - then if the policy visits a new state - asks the expert as to what action should have been taken - and this new data sample is then added into the existing dataset, hence the dataset aggregation part.

Another pretty start approach is to just take the state and action where the agent is most likely to deviate to a differens state and then in the label for those state and action, add an action that takes the robot back onto the right trajectory.

What is recorded during the demonstrations - the joint positions of the leader robots (input from the human) and use them as actions. Given a frame of observations - what ACT tries to do is to predict the next k frames of actions - i.e. what the joint positions will be in the next k frames - but then the Question is  - how can joint positions be actions? 

A tiny thing to clarify or more like - reiterate here is the fact that "actions" do not literally mean the force applied in an RL system unless explicitly mentioned. It is simply the command that the learned policy issues and in the case of ALOHA and ACT these predicted points are treated as target joint position so it essentially predicts the target joint positions given each frame as input and then it is left upto the low level PID Or whatever controller to move the robotic arm to the given target configuration.

What is the effective horizon of a task - The effecive horizon is the number of sequential decisions a policy must make before completing a task - not necessarily the task's physical duration. Remember that ACT predicts a group of actions together instead of one - it is not like it replaces the 100 step individual action steps with just one giant action term.

So the policy being a conditionalk VAE means that the action that the decoder part of the network does not just sample anything  - it uses the style variable z which to which teh encoder as embedded information into and then uses that along with c (which is the sitation or class it must respect)
