## LLC Changes

An LLC transition could correspond to a new capability development or to a mechanism re-organization, but it could also be a geometric or optimization artifact that doesn't correspond to a new meaningful circuit. So we need to also somehow verify and check whether the changes in the "Landscape" that we are observing are actually relevant changes in the model's capabilities too or just random noise.


What is the difference between behavioral emergence and causal circuit emergence. Behavioral emergence is when the model starts being able to do a particular task. FOr instance a sudden jump in the accuracy could be one way in which you could identify this. Causal Circuit emergence is about when a specific internal mechanism becomes actually responsible for that behavior, as shown by intervention, not just correlation i.e. you are actually able to pinpoint and say that yea - this thing right here is responsible for the output that we just recieved.

And these definitions become the backbone of some key design choices - for instance the tasks we choose have to be reasoning based with a verifiable answer and correctness so that we can actually measure what changes are behavioral and then find the causal circuits for them. 

A good question here is - what changes in the LLC values are treated as "meaningful" spike or change and also what changes in the accuracy metric goes as a change in the metric values. 

And the way this is done in the industry currently is as follows (from the Hooland et. al paper):

The LLC values over a given period are smoothened and then we find the critical points. So as in, where the derivative with respect to log training time gets small and that can be treated as a region for further inspection on whether there was a behavioral shift there or some sort of causal trace can be found.

Smoothing the LLC basically looking for the underlying trend by removing the spikes and erratic. A possible way to smoothen that would be rolling window average. 

Now, one interesting Question here would be that when looking at behavioral changes and their correlation with a change in the LLC values - one can identify behavioral shifts just by looking at the change in the model's performance and certain metrics while it is a bit trickier to identify the causal circuits. It involves activation patching, ablations run on the various attnetion heads, and sometimes even swapping components all together to help understand whether a particular subnetwork is causally necessary, not just whether it's active.

Now instead of LLC shifts just lining up with the behavioral or causal changes but whether these can help us predict these events?

A brief reminder on the thing we are actually targetting and their meaning.

So LLC - Local Learning Coefficient changes as the model undergoes training and learns. And we are trying to see if the timing of the change in these LLC values corresponds to "behavioral" changes. Now when you put it that way, it seems it has something to do with the nature of the model, but by definition behavioral change the kind that we are working with seems to only involve the capability of the model that can be measured via a verifiable metric or a value. That is it, there is no internal peeking of any kind involved here. 

While on the other hand the causal mechanism analysis peeks into the internals of the model and checks what components in the model architecture are actually responsible for this new capability if at all they are responsible - as there are multiple scenarios that can develop here - the LLC may or may not correspond to any new capabilities at all, and the new capabilty may or may not have a circuit associated with it at all etc.
